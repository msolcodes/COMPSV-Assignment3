Question 1: Why is a stack the right choice for undo/redo?

A stack is the better choice for an undo/redo because it makes the last addition be the first out. 
It's like a stack of books; the top book on the stack is the first one to take off. 
So, if the user makes several changes and they select undo, 
the last change they made will be the first one that is undone. 
This is efficient because it follows the natural order that matches how undo/redo works.

Question 2: Why is a queue better suited for the help desk?

Because it follows the order that the first one in is the first one out, 
like in a line of customers. The last person that arrives is at the end of the line and will be helped last, 
and the person at the front arrived first, and will be helped before anyone else. 
This is a good choice because customers are kept in the order in which they arrived, 
and the program does not have to search for who should be next.

Question 3: How do your implementations differ from Python’s built-in lists?

With built-in lists, I relied on Python’s built-in ways to store, add, and remove items. 
For this program, the stack and queue were built using nodes and pointers instead of the built-in features. 
The stack was responsible for keeping track of the top, while the queue kept track of the front and rear. 
This allowed more control over how items were added and removed.
